Dairy Nutrition, 2 min read, by Joel Cherono

Feed management: the foundation of a profitable dairy farm

Many farmers focus on buying good cows, but profitable dairy farming starts before the cow arrives on the farm. A dairy enterprise is built on three major foundations, nutrition, animal health and proper management. Among these, feeding plays one of the biggest roles because what a cow eats directly affects milk production, fertility, health and profitability.

Read full article

Why feed management matters

A productive dairy cow requires a balanced diet that provides energy, protein, minerals and enough fibre for proper digestion. Poor feeding practices can lead to:

  • Reduced milk production
  • Poor body condition
  • Reproductive challenges
  • Increased disease risks
  • Higher production costs

Farmers should understand that expensive feeds do not always mean better results. The goal is to provide the right nutrients at the right stage of production.

Plan feeds before bringing cows

One common mistake among new dairy farmers is purchasing animals before preparing a feeding plan. Before starting a dairy project, farmers should consider:

  • Availability of quality feeds throughout the year
  • Feed storage options
  • Silage preparation
  • Feed formulation
  • Water availability

A successful dairy farm begins with a feeding strategy, not just buying animals.

The role of silage preparation

Silage is an important feed resource, especially when farmers want consistent feed supply. One of the most important steps during silage preparation is proper compaction. Good compaction:

  • Removes oxygen
  • Improves fermentation
  • Prevents spoilage
  • Maintains feed quality

Small mistakes during silage preparation can lead to major feed losses.

Final thoughts

Healthy cows produce better when they receive proper nutrition and management. At Sochoi Dairies Ltd, Joel Cherono continues to share practical approaches that help farmers improve feeding strategies and build sustainable dairy enterprises.

Calf Management, 2 min read, by Joel Cherono

Calf rearing: building the future of your dairy herd

A successful dairy farm does not start with a mature cow. It starts with a healthy calf. The way calves are managed during their early stages determines their future growth, productivity and overall performance.

Read full article

Proper calf management requires attention from birth until the animal joins the milking herd.

Stage one: birth and colostrum management

The first days of a calf's life are extremely important. During this stage farmers should focus on:

  • Providing quality colostrum immediately after birth
  • Proper navel care
  • Maintaining hygiene
  • Monitoring calf health

Colostrum provides essential antibodies that help protect calves from diseases.

Stage two: early rearing

Between 2 to 8 weeks, calves require proper nutrition to support growth. Important areas include:

  • Milk or milk replacers
  • Starter feeds
  • Clean water
  • Mineral supplementation

Introducing calves to solid feeds early helps prepare them for the transition after weaning.

Stage three: weaning period

The transition period between 9 and 12 weeks requires careful management. Farmers should gradually reduce milk feeding while ensuring calves continue consuming quality starter feeds, hay, minerals and adequate water. Abrupt changes can stress calves and affect growth.

Building better dairy businesses

Investing in calf management is investing in the future of your herd. Every productive cow started as a calf that required proper care, nutrition and attention.

Breeding and Genetics, 2 min read, by Joel Cherono

Choosing the right dairy breed: why genetics matter

Choosing dairy breeds is one of the biggest decisions a farmer makes. However, selecting a cow should not only be based on appearance or body size. Good genetics, production history and health records play a major role in determining long term performance.

Read full article

Beyond physical appearance

A large cow does not automatically mean higher production. Farmers should consider:

  • Family production history
  • Fertility performance
  • Disease resistance
  • Adaptability to local conditions

The best breed is one that matches your farm environment and management ability.

The importance of records

Modern dairy farming requires proper record keeping. Records help farmers track milk production, breeding performance, health history and growth rates. Technology solutions such as digital herd management systems can help farmers make better decisions using accurate information.

Final thoughts

Successful dairy farming is a combination of good genetics, proper nutrition and excellent management. Choosing the right animals is the beginning, but how you manage them determines the results.
